## What's new

- **A `temple` command line.** `temple sessions ls` lists the sessions open in Temple; `new`, `open` and `close` start, resume and close them; `temple projects` groups them by folder; and `temple history ls|search|get` covers every session, open, closed, archived or never imported. Every command takes `--json`. `new` and `open` start Temple if it isn't running, and `close` refuses an agent that is still working unless you pass `--force`. Homebrew links it onto your PATH.
- **History holds everything, archive included.** Archived sessions are a scope of History rather than a place of their own. Archive a whole selection at once, with undo; ⌘Z after a Restore undoes the Restore. ⌘Y and ⌘⇧Y open History and stay there. The page counts what it lists, names sessions whose transcript is gone, and loads faster.
- **Temple archives sessions nobody can resume.** When a session's transcript or folder is gone, Temple archives it and says so once, with Undo. It acts only on proof: a complete listing of the store, or a missing folder on a mounted volume. A failed scan, a missing store or an unplugged drive never counts. Every automatic archive records why, and Restore always brings the session back.
- **Every surface works from Temple's own record of a session.** The sidebar, palette, launcher, tabs and History all read the session's row instead of re-parsing transcripts, so a session whose transcript is gone still shows up, opens in its own folder with its own agent, and can be archived. File watching does less work per change.
- **⌘K keeps what you type.** Keys typed straight after ⌘K land in the palette, not the terminal behind it; Return opens the highlighted row; while a panel is open, only Esc and real shortcuts reach the window. Search finds a renamed session by its original title too.
- **Title bar and scrolling.** Double-clicking empty title bar zooms everywhere, over the sidebar too, and only when both clicks land on empty band. The launcher and Settings scroll when they are taller than the window, without drawing into the title bar.
- **Clearer failures.** A failed startup says what stopped it. A tab whose folder is gone says what to do, links to History and offers Archive. A row whose transcript is gone is dimmed, and its error says so.

## Upgrading

- The first launch migrates the database (columns added, nothing removed), and an older Temple can still open it. Your existing archives stay yours.
- On that first launch Temple may archive sessions whose transcript or folder is already gone. The notice offers Undo, and History ▸ Archived restores any of them.
- The old index cache is retired once; it was a cache and holds nothing you lose.
